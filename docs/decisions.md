## 2026-09-12 — Merge the loose plates into the hull segments: 13 parts and 8 plates down to 6 and 4

`merge-the-loose-plates-into-the-hull-segments-13`

The transom, both bulkheads, the centre girder, the shaft seat and two of the three deck panels stopped being separate parts and are now printed as part of the hull segment they used to be glued to. Each hull segment prints on a transverse face with its one solid cross-wall ON THE BED, which is what makes the merge possible at all: put a cross-wall at the far end of a segment and it becomes a 186 mm horizontal plate at the top of the print.

**Rejected**

- **merging the STEM CAP into hull_bow** — at the top of a bulkhead-down print it roofs the open cavity. fdm.bridge_span measured an 85 mm unsupported span across 2649 mm2. Printing that segment stem-down only moves the problem to the bulkhead, which is 132 mm wide rather than 85. It stays a separate 69 x 39 mm plate.
- **merging the BOW DECK into hull_bow** — it prints fine as a longitudinal wall, but hull_bow will not fuse to a single body, and as a separate body inside the same part its underside roofs the cavity with nothing in the same body beneath it: fdm.bridge_span, 19 mm, UNANCHORED. As its own part it lies flat on the bed and the question does not arise. Rejecting it also keeps the bow compartment open until the foam and the interior epoxy are in, and sealed air with no foam fails closed-loop.
- **putting each bulkhead on the segment it closes** — bulkhead_aft belongs to hull_mid and bulkhead_fwd to hull_bow, not to hull_aft and hull_mid. A bulkhead only merges into the segment whose print puts it on the BED.
- **a boolean union to make each merged part a single solid** — attempted, measured and rejected. Blender unions the bodies correctly by volume (0.95-0.98 of the inputs) but the result never survives the normalisation cad-solid applies on load: 2 non-manifold edges and 11 degenerate faces on hull_aft. manifold3d returns a watertight body in milliseconds whose STL round-trip is not watertight. geometry.fuse still tries and its guard still catches it, so the parts ship as overlapping bodies.

**Params:** `plate_proud_mm`, `merge_bite_mm`  
**Claims:** `C7`, `P1`

WHY IT IS AN IMPROVEMENT, not a convenience: a watertight bulkhead printed integral with its hull has no bond line to fail, and watertightness is the top physical risk on this boat (claim P1, one of five only water can settle). Seven glued joints became two.

WHAT IT COST, stated:
* Printed mass 480 g -> 516 g and print time 19.8 h -> 21.3 h. About 10 g of that is not real: overlapping bodies double-count their overlap, which inflates the modelled mass by roughly 2% in the conservative direction. The other ~26 g is: the integral decks are 2.0 mm where the loose panels were 1.4 mm, and the plates now cover the full section and stand 0.3 mm proud instead of being inset.
* An integral bulkhead can no longer be epoxy-filleted from both sides. It does not need to be, because it has no joint, but the hull-to-hull seam beside it is now filleted from one side only.
* hull_mid ships as seven overlapping bodies rather than one solid, and cad.wall_thickness measures 0.24 mm across an internal face between two of them. That gate was already failing before this change, on deck_mid, for the same reason.

Loaded freeboard moved 27.3 mm -> 26.5 mm and trim -1.22 -> -1.27 degrees on the extra 39 g. Both still pass. No claim that passed before this change fails after it.

## 2026-09-12 — Print every part standing on a transverse face or flat; no supports except at the stem

`print-every-part-standing-on-a-transverse-face-o`

Hull segments print standing on a transverse cut face. The section changes slowly along a hull this slender, so every wall is within about 27 degrees of vertical and nothing needs support. Plates and decks print flat. The exception is hull_bow, which wants about 450 mm2 of support at the stem taper.

**Rejected**

- **keel down** — the hull bottom is near-flat and the keel has rocker, so all but one line of it is an unsupported near-horizontal overhang. Unprintable.
- **deck down** — the same flat bottom, now a roof at the top of the print. Needs support over roughly 200 x 160 mm.
- **split on the centreline** — prints beautifully with zero support and puts a 400 mm glue seam along the keel: underwater, in the highest-stress region, and the single worst place on the boat to have a seam. Rejected on watertightness, claim P1, not on printability.
- **closed-ended hull segments** — a closed tube printed axially has to bridge its own section at one end, which is 160 mm of unsupported span. That is why the transom, both bulkheads and the stem are separate flat plates rather than integral.

**Params:** `bed_x_mm`  
**Claims:** `C7`

This decision is the reason the part count is thirteen rather than six. Every one of the extra parts is a flat plate that exists so that no hull segment has to roof itself over.

## 2026-09-12 — Fill the trim gap with closed-form arithmetic, not a solver

`fill-the-trim-gap-with-closed-form-arithmetic-no`

fluids-analytic names longitudinal trim as its own nearest omission. Rather than reaching for CFD, the gap is closed in model/hullform.flotation: two unknowns (the waterline height and its slope), two equations (displacement equals mass, centre of buoyancy under centre of gravity), nested bisection, a few milliseconds. It is exactly the sequence docs/EXTENSION_PROTOCOL.md asks for -- name the quantity, classify it, try the analytic answer FIRST, and only reach for a solver if the closed form cannot answer.

**Rejected**

- **install a CFD solver** — two gigabytes, an afternoon of setup, a meshing skill, and fifteen minutes a run, to answer a question a page of algebra answers exactly. Static trim is hydrostatics, not fluid dynamics: there is no flow in it. A solver here would be slower, less accurate and unfalsifiable.
- **use LCG-LCB offset as a proxy and skip the solve** — it was the first version and it is a proxy for the thing that matters rather than the thing. The number a builder can act on is the trim ANGLE and the freeboard left after it, and the offset does not give either without the solve.
- **ignore trim, since three hydrostatic gates were already green** — which is the failure the pack warns about by name. On this boat trim costs 3.8 mm of the lowest deck-edge freeboard, 15% of the claim. Level flotation reports 31.1 mm and the boat floats at 27.3 mm.

**Params:** `max_trim_deg`  
**Claims:** `C15`

Consequence worth stating: because the solve exists, the battery strap is a design feature. Sliding the battery is the trim adjustment, and boat.trim is what says how far.

## 2026-09-12 — Raise the build budget from 200 to 400 USD

`raise-the-build-budget-from-200-to-400-usd`

The 200 USD budget was mine, not the user s: the brief said Amazon-orderable and beginner-friendly and never named a number. With real prices for parts that meet the specs, the bill is 374.50 USD. bom.cost failed against 200 and that failure is the reason this decision exists rather than a number quietly edited into the model.

**Rejected**

- **200 USD, by cutting parts** — the three lines that would have to go are the ones the engineering depends on. The RC4WD 80-turn motor (22 USD) is 12 USD dearer than a stock 380 and is the whole reason the propeller turns at 4450 rpm instead of 22000. The Savox IP67 servo (40 USD) is 34 USD dearer than an MG90S and is the one component guaranteed to meet water. The Flysky set (54 USD) has no cheaper credible alternative. Cutting them buys 80 USD and costs the boat.
- **leave the budget at 200 and report the overrun** — the budget is a claim in the ledger, and a claim nobody intends to meet is noise in every future sweep. Either it moves with a reason or it is not a budget.

**Params:** `budget_usd`  
**Claims:** `C12`

FLAG FOR THE USER: this is the one number in the project that is purely my invention. If 400 USD is wrong, change budget_usd in model/boat.py and re-run; bom.cost will tell you what has to go. The 374.50 USD also carries +/-25%: Amazon refused every product-page fetch during sourcing, so only eight of nineteen lines have a price verified on a fetchable page.

## 2026-09-12 — Three compartments, a longitudinal centre girder, and closed-cell foam in the ends

`three-compartments-a-longitudinal-centre-girder`

The equipment bay is bounded by two watertight transverse bulkheads, leaving a sealed bow compartment and a sealed stern compartment. A longitudinal centre girder runs the length of the equipment bay bilge, dividing it into two half-width cells. Both sealed end compartments are filled with closed-cell foam.

**Rejected**

- **one open hull interior** — the free-surface correction on an undivided 163 mm wide bilge is i/V = 278 mm against a solid GM of 74 mm. Three millimetres of water in the bilge and the boat is upside down. This is not a margin problem, it is a sign problem.
- **transverse bulkheads only, no centre girder** — free-surface second moment is L*b^3/12: transverse bulkheads cut L, which is LINEAR, while a longitudinal division cuts b, which is CUBIC. One centre girder divides the effect by four; three transverse bulkheads divide it by three at ten times the cost in mass and print time.
- **sealed air compartments with no foam** — fluids lens 5: sealed air volumes fail closed-loop. One crack in a printed bow compartment and the entire reserve buoyancy is gone at the moment it is needed. Closed-cell foam fails gracefully and costs about two dollars of pool noodle.
- **a bilge pump** — an order of magnitude more cost, mass, current and failure modes than a bulkhead, and it does nothing about free surface while it is pumping.

**Params:** `bulkhead_positions`, `girder_height_mm`  
**Claims:** `C17`, `C18`

Every one of these came out of packs/fluids-analytic/lenses.md, run before any geometry existed. None of it would have shown up in a gate sweep, because fluid.metacentric reports solid GM and has no concept of a compartment.

## 2026-09-12 — Scale the traced hull to LOA 420 mm; keep the shape exactly

`scale-the-traced-hull-to-loa-420-mm-keep-the-sha`

The hull as drawn at LOA 300 mm displaces 73 g at the waterline the design tool drew. The parts alone are ~265 g and the printed shell another ~130 g, so the drawn waterline is a styling line, not a loading condition. Scaling the traced offsets by 1.40 (LOA 420 mm, deck beam 163 mm, depth 65.5 mm) buys displacement as the cube of scale while the electronics mass stays fixed, and lands the loaded waterline at roughly 20 mm draft in a 65.5 mm deep hull. The hull FORM is unchanged: every offset is the traced one times 1.40, so the boat the user liked is the boat they get, bigger.

**Rejected**

- **1.00 (LOA 300 mm as drawn)** — displaces 73 g at the drawn DWL and about 400 g at 23 mm draft, which leaves ~15 mm of freeboard with a +/-15% mass estimate riding on it. It floats, but C2 (25 mm freeboard) fails and there is no margin for the mass estimate being wrong. Also does not fit a 220 mm bed in one piece anyway, so it does not even buy a single-piece print.
- **1.50 (LOA 450 mm)** — hull halves come out 225 mm long and no longer fit a 220x220 bed, which breaks the "buildable at home" constraint for the most common printer class. 1.40 gives 210 mm halves, which fit with 10 mm to spare.
- **keep LOA 300 and cut mass instead** — the floor is real: the smallest usable 2S pack is ~75 g, a 370-class motor ~55 g, and the printed shell scales with surface area not with wishes. ~390 g is the floor and it still leaves 15 mm of freeboard.
- **redraw the hull deeper at LOA 300** — the user said they like this hull profile. Changing the form is a bigger change than changing the scale, and it throws away the one piece of evidence the project has.

**Params:** `hull_scale`  
**Claims:** `C1`, `C2`, `C3`  
**Evidence:** `hull-profile`

Displacement scales as s^3 and printed shell mass as roughly s^2, while motor, ESC, servo, receiver and battery do not scale at all. That is the whole argument: going up in size is the cheapest way to buy freeboard on a boat whose payload is fixed by what you can buy. The cost is filament and print hours, both of which the fdm gates will price.
